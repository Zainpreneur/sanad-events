The name

Sanad means the pillar you lean on

In Arabic, sanad is a chain of trust — the thing that holds weight without complaining. We started in a 200 m² store in Al Rawdah with four folding chairs and a borrowed generator. Twelve years later we run a 3,500 m² warehouse and a 3,500 m² production yard in Jeddah, a 42-person planning office, and a 120-strong setup crew who can dress a 2,000-guest ballroom in a single night.

Everything sits under one roof on purpose. Your planner, your décor lead, your sound engineer and your supply foreman speak in the same morning stand-up, walk the same venue survey together, and sign the same production sheet. Nobody passes a problem down the line.

    One accountable team

    A single producer owns your event from the first call to the final strike. No handovers, no re-explaining, no blame.

  • 02

    Owned inventory, not rented hope

    3,500 m² of our own stock, serviced weekly. If it is on the proposal it is physically in our yard the morning you ask.

  • 03

    Climate-true Jeddah dates

    Every outdoor date is modelled for heat, wind and dust load — shading, airflow, cold-chain and power redundancy included.

How it runs

Five steps, one production sheet

  1. 01

    Discovery call

    30 minutes on call or at the office. Date, guest count, venue, mood, budget band, the one thing you refuse to compromise on.

  2. 02

    Concept & proposal

    Moodboards, floorplans, a supplier-by-supplier budget and three package options — inside 5 working days.

  3. 03

    Design & sourcing

    Technical drawings, custom builds in our yard, fabric and floral tests, venue permits, power and rigging sign-off.

  4. 04

    Production day

    Load-out at your access window, a technical rehearsal, a show call at T−3 hours, then we hold the floor until the last guest leaves.

  5. 05

    Strike & aftercare

    Night shift strike, inventory check, waste out, venue handover signed, itemised rental statement and a 7-day follow-up call.

Before you ask

Questions we get every week

How far in advance should we book?

Fridays and Saturdays between October and April are usually reserved 6 to 9 months out, so start at 9 months if your date is fixed. Midweek and summer dates open up 8 to 10 weeks out. Expo season in February and March needs 4 months for custom booth builds.

What are the payment terms?

25% books your date, 40% is due once the design and production sheet are signed, and the balance is settled 5 days before the event. Cards, bank transfer and approved instalments of up to 4 are all accepted. Every payment is documented on a ZATCA-compliant invoice.

Can we use our own caterer or suppliers?

Yes. Bring your caterer, photographer or entertainment and we will integrate them into the schedule, the power plan and the crew briefing. One condition: every external supplier needs an insurance certificate and a Jeddah-approved vendor registration, and they work under our floor manager on the night.

Do you deliver outside Jeddah?

We deliver across the Kingdom. Jeddah, Makkah and Dammam are standard; other cities carry a crew-travel and freight line on the proposal, quoted in advance so there are no surprises on the invoice. Very large builds outside Jeddah are quoted case by case.

What happens if we need to move the date?

Move it more than 45 days out and your deposit transfers to the new date once. Inside 45 days we will try, but that date may be gone — in that case the deposit becomes credit valid for twelve months. Cancellations inside 14 days are non-refundable because crew, freight and custom builds are already committed.
